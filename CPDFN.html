Project Introduction

The Chipewyan Prairie Dene First Nation (CPDFN) OSM camera program started in 2022 with a focus of recording land users accessing the lands near Janvier who were not members of the community. Over the years the camera program has adapted to answer different questions and concerns expressed by the community, including understanding how wildlife abundances change in proximity to human disturbance (including oil sands industry disturbance). This webpage will focus on the results from both the Human Land Use and Wildlife Abundance study designs.

Human Land Use Study

CPDFN members have been experiencing the impacts of oil sands development for a long time. Land users have seen firsthand how these developments are changing the way they access and use the land. Some of the harvesting sites have been physically lost. Others are still there, but getting to them has become harder—either because access is blocked, uncomfortable, or just not appropriate anymore.

At the same time, they’re seeing more and more outsiders—like oil sands workers staying in camps, or non-Indigenous hunters and recreational users—coming onto their land. They’re using roads and trails built for oil sands development to get into areas used traditionally for harvesting and cultural activities. This has led to increased competition for land and resources, and sometimes even dangerous or racist interactions, vandalism, littering, and poaching. It’s not just about the physical space—CPDFN members are also dealing with loud noise, bad smells, heavy traffic, bright lights, and other things that ruin the peaceful experience of being out on the land.

CPDFN has been raising these concerns with oil sands companies for years, but often, they don’t take them seriously. So CPDFN decided to collect their own evidence. From 2022 to 2024, led by Stuart Janvier, Louie Janvier, Caiden Morice and Ave Dersche with support from ABMI, CPDFN ran a camera monitoring project to track who’s coming into their territory and what kinds of impacts they’re seeing. This section of the report shares the results of that project and helps show what they already knew—yes, oil sands development has created pathways for outsiders to come onto their land, and yes, it’s causing real problems for their community.

Wildlife Abundance Study

In 2024 the cameras used in the Human Land Use study were retrieved and then deployed along the 20th Baseline to monitor wildlife. The 20th Baseline runs west to east with the Black Gold Sag-D plant to the West (high oil sands disturbance) and the proposed Dillon River Wildland with considerably less disturbance to the east (some old cutlines and occasional conventional well pads). The objective of this study is to document and compare the wildlife abundances calculated from the images collected from each camera deployed along the baseline to see if wildlife use the landscape differently when closer or further away from the area of high disturbance.

Unfortunately during the spring of 2023 a large wildfire burned through the area and a number of cameras were lost. Cameras were re-deployed afterwards and the program continued.

Camera Refresh and Image Processing

The human land use cameras were refreshed (given new SD cards and batteries) in the field by CPDFN in December 2022. The images from the SD cards were uploaded and tagged on the online platform WildTrax. WildTrax is an online platform developed by the Alberta Biodiversity Monitoring Institute for users of environmental sensors such as remote cameras to provide support with processing, organizing, and sharing data.

CPDFN have 4 projects available on WildTrax with the images stored and tagged:

  • CPDFN Wildlife Cameras: Contains images from the 18 cameras initially deployed for the Human Land Use study in January 2022 and refreshed in November later that year.
  • CPDFN Wildlife Cameras 2023: Contains images from the same 18 cameras over the next year (November 2022 to November 2023). Also includes a new location that monitors a salt lick identified by the community that is highly trafficked by wildlife.
  • CPFN 20th Baseline Cameras: This project contains images from 13 cameras deployed for the Wildlife Abundance project along 20th baseline.
  • 20th Baseline Cameras 2025: This project contains images from 20 cameras deployed further along 20th baseline for the Wildlife Abundance portion of the project.

Note that viewers will need to have a WildTrax account with the appropriate permissions from the community and be logged in to see the these images.

Camera Locations

The following interactive map can be used to explore the locations of the cameras deployed by Chipewyan Prairie Dene First Nation. Camera locations for the Human Land Use study were chosen by the community to reflect areas where monitoring was desired. Locations for the Wildlife Abundance study were chosen to assess how a gradient of land use and industry disturbance impacts wildlife distribution and abundance.

Click each camera location to see it’s name, the study it belongs to, and when it was deployed and refreshed. Cameras that belong to the Human Land Use study are coloured light blue, and those that belong to the Wildlife Abundance Study are coloured white. You can use the check boxes in the top right of the map to turn on/off the satellite imagery as well as the camera location points.



Deployment and Retrieval Dates

Figure 1 below displays the dates when each camera location was deployed and when the SD cards were retrieved (or the last day of image collection if the camera failed). These dates are marked with a point in the plot.

Deployment first took place for the Human Land Use study in February 2022, and cameras were serviced for the first time at the end of November in the same year. These cameras were retrieved from their locations in the field in November the following year, totalling nearly 2 years of monitoring.

A subset of the cameras (9) for the Wildlife Abundance study were first deployed in March 2023. This initial batch was collected and additional locations were added in February/March of 2024. Those cameras were serviced one year later in March 2025.
